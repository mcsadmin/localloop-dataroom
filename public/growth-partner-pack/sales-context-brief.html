1. What Local Loop Is (and Isn't)

The one-liner: Local Loop Merseyside is a B2B clearing-based business network for SMEs in the Liverpool City Region. The core mechanism cancels circular debt ‘loops’ freeing up cash without new money entering the system.

How clearing works: If A owes B £1,000, B owes C £1,000, and C owes A £1,000 — that's three debts totalling £3,000 but a net of zero. Clearing finds and settles those loops. Banks have done this since the 1770s. Local Loop is applying it to local SME supply chains.

Critical distinctions (for messaging accuracy):

Clearing does not = barter, blockchain, or traditional banking. Naming what Local Loop isn't activates those associations, framing should be proactive and positional, never reactive or myth-busting.

The product is a network, not a standalone tool. Value scales with density.

The problem it solves: Late payment is systemic, not individual. 68% of UK SMEs experience it. It drives roughly 20% of insolvencies. Knock-on insolvencies often follow within 6 months. The businesses most embedded in local supply chains, the ones contributing most to the local economy, are the most exposed. We are trying to flip this on its head.

The deeper value proposition:

Systemic risk analysis in supply networks — we can find where insolvency is likely and impactful and make recommendations for early resolution.

Brokering new trades that create clearing loops and reduce debt while increasing trade and circularity.

Identifying unfilled niches in the local economy

Reducing dependence on expensive finance (overdrafts, invoice factoring - i.e → leakage)

Economic incentive to collaborate — material rewards, not just rhetoric

3. What's Been Tried — Sales & Outreach History

Founding Member Recruitment

Method: Personal relationships, direct outreach from Tom

What worked: Direct, personal communication. Effective with existing contacts. The Feb 5 founding members event had good attendance and energy. Everyone committed to referring 2-3 contacts.

What didn't work: Follow through: Dil and Tom did some Founding Member onboarding sessions, asking for referrals, collected a few, made some initial contacts, but did not sufficiently commit. D&T do not consider this approach to be a failure (only their follow-through), but that the approach is still to be validated. As of March 19, only 2 actual referrals from the FM base. D&T consider the FMs still as supportive as ever, and that they will be receptive to an experienced growth partner picking things up.

Sales Training & Process Design (Jan 2026)

Team went through sales training sessions with Elite Closing Academy

Reviewed prospecting scripts, methods, qualifying questions, frameworks

Created several client journey flowcharts.

Developed qualifying questions for initial outreach and stages.

Practised role-playing sales calls.

Key learning: The scripts and frameworks exist, seem sound and applicable but have not been consistently executed or tested by the founders/team. The sales training was very valuable - Founders now hope that they have a good understanding of what a good sales process looks like, what sales needs (essentially, good sales should be to customers who already have a feeling that the product is likely to be something they want - marketing, lead gen and qualifying should aim to achieve this before sales. Founders have accepted that they are not going to become salespeople themselves.

LinkedIn Outreach Pipeline

Built a CRM in Notion with pipeline stages: Identified → Following & Commenting → DM Sent → Invited → Insight Asked → Prospect Call Booked → Discovery → Proposal Sent → Won/Lost

4-week cadence designed around CFO Circle model

Target segments: 50 accountants/fractional CFOs, 50 finance managers at local SMEs, 25 agencies & consultancies

Green-lit in March, outreach began, but execution so far limited by bandwidth - continuing given new leads in the last few weeks.

Previous Sales Role (Davie)

Davie was the closest thing to a dedicated sales function previously. This was autumn 2024 - when we were trying to sell a dream - no working tech. Went on far too long before we realised what wasn't working. We have improved ops and handoff significantly since then.

Key feedback from Davie's review:

Nothing to actually 'sell'. Biggest gap was data — the team was "working blind" without analytics

Many people signed up but had minimal contact after — majority of time was re-engagement

Once a sale is made, account management should take over — avoid splitting sales time (this was from a very 'seat-of-the-pants salesman. We actively disagree in the case of Local Loop — sales needs to move from early relationship sales with segue into customer relationship management, future sales should be anonymous via website as a result of reputation, referrals from members, accountants, bookkeepers etc. Long-term the key is customer retention, so divorcing sales (bums on seats) from service - providing value, making members feel valued seems a mistake.

Personal content performed best.

Educational content was less memorable

Without analytics, insights were based on gut feel, not data

Need structured handoffs: sign-off meeting between sales and back-office when someone joins

Sometimes unclear if someone has officially joined — process not scalable

5. Content & Marketing — What Exists, What's Worked

LinkedIn presence (90-day programme planned, Mar 2 – May 1 actually):

Cadence: Monday carousels (Pillar A: Cashflow Reality or similar), Wednesday insights (Pillar B: Clearing Explained), Friday community posts (FM spotlights, partner co-posts)

Voice: Company posts = professional, punchy, warm. Founder posts (Tom/Dil) = distinct personal voice.

Phase as of April 2026: Authority Building — proof-based content is parked until clearing results land.

What worked (from Davie review + content programme):

Personal content outperformed educational content

FM video endorsement posts generate engagement

Counterintuitive hooks ("debt can cancel itself") get saves and shares

Polls generate engagement signals but need DM follow-up to convert

Data-driven content (LCR payment data) differentiates from generic business advice

Key content/messaging principles learned:

Don't plant objections — naming what you're not activates those associations

Credibility should be built, but until then, borrowed — partner authority (accountants, legal) does the work.

"Might catch up" not "will catch up" — r.e regulations/competitors - preserve urgency for joining now.

Personal stories over templates — FM posts should feel differentiated

Accuracy is non-negotiable — verify specific figures before publishing

Be delicate about "unmatched market intelligence" claims — could compromise trust around sensitive data submission

7. Strategic Context for the Salesperson

What the founders have acknowledged:

They are not salespeople and won't become them soon

The referral-led growth model has not delivered at the pace needed

The previous MVP has been sunsetted, AI-led tech development for new funnel tools is next

A professional salesperson is needed to design and co-design the entire sales process

What the salesperson is walking into:

A product with genuine differentiated value but no proven sales motion

A small but real founding member base with video testimonials

A solid LinkedIn presence and infrastructure

Good strategic documentation but limited execution data

No formal objection handling, tested pricing, or conversion metrics

Strong brand and content foundations to build on

What the salesperson should probably shape (draft):

The ICP definition

The sales process end-to-end — the client journey flowchart exists but hasn't been battle-tested

Pricing and packaging — founding member terms are set, but general membership pricing needs market validation

The handoff between marketing and sales — currently undefined

What "account management" looks like post-sale
