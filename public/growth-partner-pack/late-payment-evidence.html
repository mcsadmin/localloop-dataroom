Late Payment Evidence Pack

Liverpool City Region and national UK data on late payment to SMEs. This is the evidence base underpinning Local Loop's core value proposition and is suitable for use in sales conversations and partner presentations.

Purpose: Verified, attributable statistics for a one-page sales asset for SMEs in the Liverpool City Region. Every figure below carries a source, publisher, date and a robustness flag.

Inter-council obligations from public spending data published by the six LCR councils (Source: Tom).

TL;DR

The single strongest national figure is from the UK government: late payment costs the UK economy almost £11 billion a year, closes roughly 38 businesses every day (~14,000 a year), and leaves ~£26 billion owed to businesses at any one time — published by the Department for Business and Trade (DBT) on 31 July 2025 and based on a ~1,455-business econometric study by London Economics (YouGov/IFF survey). This is the most authoritative, most citable number set available and should anchor any relevant assets.

Source: Office of the Small Business Commissioner / DBT: Late Payments Research (smallbusinesscommissioner.gov.uk/late-payments-research-2/ and assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/688a089a6478525675738ff9/late_payments_research_impact_on_uk_economy.pdf).

Genuinely local late-payment data is thin. The best Liverpool-specific figure is FreeAgent's postcode analysis: 54.6% of invoices to Liverpool ("L" postcode) small businesses were paid late in June 2023–June 2024 (down from 61% in 2022-23 and 66% in 2019-20, when Liverpool was the UK's worst area). This is a postcode cut of a national software dataset, not a survey of Liverpool City Region firms — use it as "Liverpool-area" colour, not robust regional research, and pair it with the region's insolvency exposure (the North West recorded the UK's highest insolvency-related activity in 2024–25).

For the human/business cost, the most defensible national figures are: businesses spend ~86 hours a year chasing each late account (DBT/Small Business Commissioner, 2025); 45% of small firms report more late payments than a year ago and 24% wait up to 60 extra days (FSB/GoCardless, March 2025); and Xero's hard transaction data shows UK small firms were paid 8.4 days late on average across 2025. (Source: GoCardless)

Source Quality Ranking — What to Cite First

The hierarchy below ranks figures by how safely they can be put in front of a prospect. Tier 1 is government/primary-source data you can cite without qualification. Tier 2 is reputable named research worth citing with attribution. Tier 3 is directional or older material to use only with caveats.

Tier 1 — Cite freely (UK government / primary source):

Late payment costs the UK economy almost £11 billion per year (~0.4% of GDP); ~14,000 businesses close each year (38 a day) as a direct result; ~£26 billion is owed to businesses at any one time (avg ~£17,000 per business affected); over 1.5 million businesses (28%) are affected each year; affected businesses spend on average 86 hours a year chasing (133 million hours economy-wide). Source: DBT / Office of the Small Business Commissioner / London Economics, "Late Payments Research: Estimating the total economic impact of late payments in the UK," 31 July 2025 (survey n≈1,455 via YouGov/IFF). Verbatim:"Late payments are estimated to cost the UK economy almost £11 billion per year. 14,000 businesses close each year because of late payments, equivalent to 38 businesses every day… Businesses are owed an estimated £26 billion in late payments at any given time, on average £17,000 per business affected."The £26bn estimate has a stated 90% confidence interval of £18bn–£36bn — quote the £26bn but know the range. (Source: Small Business Commissioner)

Tier 2 — Cite with attribution (named, recent, reputable):

45% of small firms report more late payments than a year ago; 50% expect them to rise; 24% wait up to 60 extra days. Source: FSB / GoCardless Late Payments Report, 6 March 2025 (online survey, n=2,298, fieldwork Oct–Dec 2024). (Note: a separate FSB 2025 figure widely reported is that 54% of small firms were paid late in the past three months.) (Sources: GoCardless; Francis Wilks & Jones)

UK small businesses were paid 8.4 days late on average across 2025 (8.0 days in the Dec 2025 quarter), against an average time-to-be-paid of ~29 days — a near-record low for the series. Source: Xero Small Business Insights, published 26 Feb 2026 (anonymised transaction data from Xero's UK customer base — behavioural, not survey). (Source: Xero)

62% of UK small businesses had unpaid invoices, owed £21,400 each on average; 54% had invoices 30+ days overdue. Source: Intuit QuickBooks 2025 UK Small Business Late Payments Report (quickbooks.intuit.com/uk/blog/small-business-late-payments-report-2025/).

Tier 3 — Use with explicit caveats (older, advocacy, or methodology-limited):

"50,000 business closures a year" and "average SME owed £22,000" — the widely-quoted FSB / Smart Data Foundry figures. Still circulated by government in 2024 but now effectively superseded by the more rigorous 2025 DBT £11bn / 14,000-closures study. Prefer the DBT numbers.

FreeAgent's Liverpool/postcode late-payment percentages (see regional section) — directional, postcode-level, not a local survey.

Two data points are especially persuasive for a netting/clearing pitch because they describe gridlock up the supply chain. Exactly the problem clearing solves:

DBT's 2025 research found payment problems multiply the further down a supply chain you go, and that 24% of firms said incorrectly-handled invoices added to delays. (Source: Business Money)

36% of small businesses say late incoming payments stop them paying their own suppliers on time, and 18% say it hits their ability to pay employees (FSB/GoCardless, Mar 2025). This "domino effect" is the core narrative for clearing mutual debt. (Source: The Fintech Times)

National human / business cost

Time: Affected businesses spend on average 86 hours a year chasing each late account — equal to 133 million hours across the economy (DBT / Small Business Commissioner, Jul 2025). An older Intuit QuickBooks estimate put the time cost at ~56.4 million hours / £6.3 billion (2018-19 study, n≈500) — usable but flag the age. (Sources: GOV.UK; QuickBooks)

Closures: ~14,000 businesses (38 a day) close each year as a direct result of late payment (DBT, Jul 2025). This replaces the older FSB "50,000 closures" claim, which was a broader cash-flow estimate.

Cash flow & borrowing: 28% of small firms have used short-term financing (loans/credit lines) to cover late-payment gaps, rising to 40% in food & beverage (FSB/GoCardless, Mar 2025). DBT estimates ~9% of all SME debt finance is taken on directly because of late payment. (Source: The Fintech Times)

Mental health: 39% of SME owners say their mental wellbeing suffered and 34% reported sleepless nights because of late payments (Barclays, Jan 2022, survey n=500). FSB separately found late/non-payment affected the wellbeing of ~a quarter of owners during the pandemic. Both are dated but are the clearest named figures on owner stress. (Source: Lancashire Evening Post)

Insolvency link (supply chain): R3 found late payment was a primary or major cause of 23% of insolvencies (R3 survey, 2016) and that over a quarter (26%) of UK companies had suffered a financial hit following the insolvency of a customer, supplier or debtor in a six-month period (R3, 2018). Concept is strong; data is old, present as "industry body R3 has long linked late payment to insolvency." (Source: R3)

Liverpool City Region / North West Data — and Its Limits

Headline honesty point for the salesperson: there is no robust, recently-published statistic collected specifically from Liverpool City Region businesses about late payment. The figures below are the best available, but most are national datasets cut to a Liverpool postcode, or regional insolvency/credit data — not local surveys. Treat them as "Liverpool-area context," and never imply they came from a dedicated local study.

Best available Liverpool-area late-payment figure (postcode cut of national data)

54.6% of invoices to Liverpool ("L" postcode) small businesses were paid late in June 2023–June 2024; within the ten largest UK cities, Liverpool was mid-table. Source: FreeAgent, Late Payment press release/blog, Aug–Sep 2024 (freeagent.com/company/press-room/late-payment-2024/ and freeagent.com/blog/best-areas-for-getting-paid/). Methodology: invoice data from ~150,000 UK FreeAgent customers; "late" = paid >3 days past the stated deadline; broken down by postcode. Robustness: moderate-to-weak as a local stat — it reflects only FreeAgent's customers in the L postcode, not the Combined Authority area, and is a slice of a national dataset. (Source: FreeAgent)

Trend (same source, comparable method): Liverpool 66% (2019-20, the UK's single worst area that year) → 61% (2022-23) → 54.6% (2024). The improving trend is real but Liverpool has historically performed worse than the UK average. FreeAgent's most recent edition (Oct 2025) put the UK average at 62.6% but did not separately report Liverpool, so no 2025 local number exists. (Source: Financial IT)

The North West region as a whole: ~49.5% of invoices paid late (FreeAgent, 2024 edition) — roughly the national average. (Source: FreeAgent)

Regional business-failure exposure (genuinely regional, robust)

The North West recorded the UK's highest number of insolvency-related activities in 2024 and again in 2025 — 4,880 cases in 2025, up 1.4% on 2024 (4,814); only Greater London had more in absolute terms (5,684). In April 2025 the North West hit a 25-month high of 473 cases (up 20.4% on April 2024) and led the entire UK, ahead of London. Source: R3 Business Health Report / Creditsafe data, reported via TheBusinessDesk and Insider Media, 2025–early 2026 (quote: Fran Henshaw, Chair of R3 in the North West). Robustness: high — administrative insolvency records. Use this to argue the region is unusually exposed to the downstream consequences of cash-flow failure, even though R3 does not attribute these specific cases to late payment. (Sources: TheBusinessDesk.com; Insider Media)

One private analysis (Helix Law, 2024) ranked the North West top for company insolvencies (~4,034 in 2024); treat as secondary/commercial research, not official. (Source: TechRound)

Market-sizing for the region (for "addressable businesses")

~92,610 active businesses in the Liverpool City Region — "the highest ever on record." Though some records estimate this to be much higher (Dil). Source: Beauhurst data, reported by Growth Platform (LCR Growth Company), Sep 2024 (growthplatform.org). SMEs make up ~99% of these (LCR Combined Authority). The official, more conservative alternative is the ONS "Liverpool City Region SME" dataset (VAT/PAYE-registered enterprises) if a government source is preferred. (Sources: Growth Platform; Liverpool City Region Combined Authority)

Local bodies — what exists and what doesn't

Liverpool & Sefton Chamber of Commerce runs the British Chambers of Commerce Quarterly Economic Survey locally, which measures cashflow — but only national QES headlines are published; no Liverpool-specific late-payment cut is in the public domain. A genuinely local number would require approaching the Chamber directly for its regional microdata. (Source: Liverpool Chamber)

Liverpool City Region Combined Authority / Growth Platform publicly welcomed the Government's 2025 late-payment crackdown and call late payment "one of the biggest barriers to small business growth," but publish no local late-payment statistic. (Source: Liverpool City Region Combined Authority)

The Best Truly-Regional Figure (North West)

The closest thing to a genuinely regional (not postcode-cut, not national) late-payment statistic comes from R3's credit-data partner Creditsafe:

North West businesses had ~1,871,844 overdue invoices on their books in Q1 2025 — up 14.8% on Q1 2024 (1,630,659) — spread across 173,664 North West companies (up 7.3% year-on-year). Source: R3 / Creditsafe regional analysis, reported via LiverpoolWorld, 2025. Robustness: good as a regional indicator (drawn from commercial credit records covering North West-registered companies), though it counts overdue invoices broadly rather than isolating Liverpool City Region alone. (Source: LiverpoolWorld)

This is the strongest figure to lead with for a regional audience because (a) it is genuinely North West data, not a national average, (b) it is recent (Q1 2025), and (c) a rising volume of overdue invoices across nearly 174,000 firms is precisely the multilateral debt gridlock a netting/clearing network exists to unwind. Pair it with the FreeAgent Liverpool trend (66% → 54.6%) for city-level colour and the R3 North West insolvency-leadership data for the consequence.

Verification note: the exact LiverpoolWorld URL for the Creditsafe Q1 2025 overdue-invoices figure was supplied via secondary reporting and should be re-confirmed against the original R3/Creditsafe release before it goes into a published asset. If it cannot be verified quickly, lead instead with the verified DBT national £11bn / 38-a-day figures plus the R3 North West insolvency data, which are fully sourced above.
